Your journey

How it works

You keep one GitHub repository for two years. Every week you open a Colab notebook, learn a concept, and apply it to your project. By the end, you have a portfolio-quality tool that you built from scratch.

There are no exams. Your grade comes from what you build, what you can explain, and the evidence you produce every week.

Year 1 — Learn to code, build a working tool

CP1 (Semester 1): You start from zero — print("hello") — and by Week 14 you have a working data pipeline. Every week adds one concept: variables, conditionals, loops, functions, file I/O. Each session is 5 hours: learn the concept, apply it to your project, run the check, submit.

CP2 (Semester 2): Your v1 pipeline works but it's fragile. Now you make it robust: validate input schemas, add configurable cleaning rules, create professional plots, generate reports, write tests, and move all code into /src. By Week 14 your tool is something you'd be proud to show.

Year 2 — Engineer it, optimize it

OOP (Semester 3): You refactor your v2 code into an architecture with components — DataSource, Cleaner, Analyzer, Plotter, Reporter. You learn design patterns (Strategy, Factory), write pytest tests, and build a plugin system. Sessions are 3 hours.

DSA (Semester 4): You find the bottleneck in your pipeline and optimize it using the right data structure — hash tables, heaps, binary search, trees. You prove the improvement with benchmarks: baseline vs optimized, Big-O analysis, timing plots. Your tool is now fast and you can explain why.

What does a typical week look like?

  1. Open the Core Notebook in Colab — learn the concept with worked examples
  2. Open your Studio Notebook — apply the concept to your project
  3. Finish the must-pass core first, then the standard target, then stretch if time
  4. Run the Universal Check — it tells you pass or fail
  5. Fix failures in /src, not in the notebook
  6. Complete the reflection and submit
  7. Do the Homework at home before next week

What if I fall behind?

Every week has three levels. If you can't finish the standard target, finish the must-pass core — that keeps you safe. If you can't even finish that, submit a recovery ticket (what failed, what you tried, what you'll do next). The instructor will help you get back on track.

You get up to 3 recovery tickets per semester with full credit. After that, missed weeks are capped at the core level. The system is designed so nobody fails silently — but you have to show up and try.

Tracks & switching

You explore all 5 tracks during Weeks 1–2 and confirm your choice by Week 3. After that, your track is locked for the rest of Year 1. Before Year 2 starts, one exceptional reset may be approved. No changes during OOP or DSA.

Grading

All tracks are graded the same way:

  • Weekly submissions + universal checks: 50%
  • Quizzes / oral explain-your-code: 20%
  • Release demo (v1 / v2 / v3): 20%
  • Professional habits (reflections, recovery, compliance): 10%

Teamwork is allowed, but understanding is individual. If your team submits passing code and you can't explain it, you get the penalty.

AI tools

If the instructor allows AI tools: you must understand and explain everything you submit. Disclose AI usage in reflections. "It runs" is not enough — you must know why it works.
